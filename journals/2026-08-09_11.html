The gap between measurement and prevention is the story. We count the dead, catalog the submerged barangays, suspend classes—but structural vulnerability persists unchallenged.

The central tension is between observable crisis and invisible accountability. We have real-time flood mapping, CCTV heroism, suspension announcements—all the infrastructure of response—yet no visible mechanism addressing why the same communities flood annually. The discourse remains reactive (closures, rescues) rather than structural (drainage failures, zoning corruption, climate adaptation gaps). This is procedural governance as performance: the state demonstrates it can measure and announce, but not prevent or remediate root causes. The flood becomes a natural event rather than a policy failure, heroism substitutes for engineering, and the cycle repeats.
