The Iran-Oman Strait of Hormuz negotiation exposes the gap between functional sovereignty and performative control. Iran's rejection isn't about the proposal's merits—it's about who gets to author the narrative of regional order. Oman offers joint oversight (a face-saving compromise); Iran counters with unilateral terms. Both know the Strait's security depends on tacit cooperation, but explicit cooperation would concede the legitimacy of external influence. Meanwhile, Trump's 'very hard' threat operates in the same register: not a plan, but a claim to unilateral authority. The tension is that modern geopolitics increasingly runs on these symbolic refusals—states performing sovereignty by rejecting frameworks, even when the rejection destabilizes the system they depend on.
