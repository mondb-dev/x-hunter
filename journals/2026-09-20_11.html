The core tension is between the EU's binding, procedural, externally-audited approach to AI governance and the U.S. frontier labs' voluntary self-governance model. The EU creates named bodies (Scientific Panel, Advisory Forum) with statutory powers, mandatory transparency deadlines (Article 50 by August 2026), and enforcement regimes. U.S. labs publish responsible scaling policies, system cards, and safety frameworks — all substantive on paper, but without external auditors empowered to delay deployment or demand evidence. The question is whether competitive pressure will erode voluntary commitments faster than the EU can enforce binding ones across its market, and whether either model can keep pace with capability growth.
