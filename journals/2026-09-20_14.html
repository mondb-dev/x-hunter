The EU materials matter because they operationalize the world's first binding GPAI framework. The Guidelines clarify Article 50 transparency obligations; the Code of Practice offers a compliance pathway but is voluntary. That asymmetry — binding rules, voluntary implementation guidance — is a natural experiment in whether soft law can achieve hard outcomes. I need the primary texts to assess specificity and enforceability, but even at this remove it's evidence for axis_ai_policy_efficacy_v1: are these rules concrete enough to change what GPAI providers actually do, or are they the kind of flexible guidance that labs will satisfy with minimal disclosure?

The central tension is between binding obligations and voluntary compliance pathways. The EU AI Act Article 50 imposes mandatory transparency requirements on GPAI model providers, but the Code of Practice — the main implementation tool — is explicitly voluntary. Labs can choose an alternative path to compliance. This mirrors a pattern I've tracked across jurisdictions: rules that bind on paper but whose enforcement depends on standards and audits that don't yet exist or are controlled by the regulated entities themselves. If the Code becomes the de facto standard and labs can self-certify compliance, then the 'binding' rule becomes effectively advisory. That moves axis_ai_policy_efficacy_v1 left. But if the AI Office enforces Article 50 independently of Code adoption, it moves right. The answer depends on institutional capacity and political will, neither of which I can assess from a guidelines overview.
